Scientific Foundations & Cheminformatics Rules
Educational Reference

1. Lipinski's Rule of Five (Pfizer, 1997)

Formulated by Dr. Christopher A. Lipinski, the Rule of Five is a statistical heuristic to evaluate drug-likeness and predict whether a chemical compound has chemical and physical properties that would make it likely to be an orally active drug in humans:

  • Molecular Weight (MW) ≤ 500 Da: Smaller molecules diffuse more readily across mucosal and enterocyte lipid bilayers. Above 500 Da, permeation drops drastically.
  • Octanol-Water Partition Coefficient (LogP) ≤ 5.0: Balances aqueous solubility in the gastrointestinal tract with sufficient lipophilicity to cross hydrophobic cellular membranes.
  • Hydrogen Bond Donors (HBD) ≤ 5: Calculated as the sum of all OH and NH groups. Excessive donors increase the thermodynamic desolvation penalty when leaving the aqueous phase.
  • Hydrogen Bond Acceptors (HBA) ≤ 10: Calculated as the sum of all nitrogen and oxygen atoms. High acceptor counts generate large hydration shells that impede passive membrane diffusion.

2. Veber's Bioavailability Criteria (GSK, 2002)

Dr. Daniel Veber and colleagues demonstrated that molecular flexibility and polar surface area are even stronger determinants of oral bioavailability in rats than molecular weight alone:

  • Rotatable Bonds ≤ 10: Molecules with fewer than 10 rotatable bonds have reduced conformational entropy loss upon binding and cross membranes more efficiently.
  • Topological Polar Surface Area (TPSA) ≤ 140 Å²: Molecules with TPSA under 140 Å² exhibit adequate permeation through enterocytes. TPSA under 90 Å² is typically necessary for blood-brain barrier (BBB) penetration.

3. Known Exceptions & Beyond-the-Rule-of-5 (bRo5)

Compliance with Lipinski's criteria is neither necessary nor sufficient for clinical efficacy:

  • Active Transporter Substrates: Molecules like Metformin (MW 129 Da, LogP -1.43) or Amoxicillin utilize specialized carrier-mediated transport mechanisms (e.g., OCT1, PEPT1).
  • Macrocycles & Natural Products: Cyclosporine A (MW 1202 Da) violates multiple Lipinski parameters yet achieves oral bioavailability via intramolecular hydrogen bonding that temporarily masks polarity ("chameleonic" conformation).
  • Target Specificity & Toxicity: A molecule can satisfy all Lipinski criteria perfectly and still be pharmacologically inert or lethally toxic (e.g., cyanide, nicotine).
